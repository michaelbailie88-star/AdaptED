🎧 What Great Listeners Actually Do
Tap each behaviour to mark it practiced. Great listening is a skill — not just staying quiet. It's two-way, constructive, and builds people up.
💡 Remember: A good listener isn't a sponge that absorbs — they're a trampoline that bounces ideas back. Two-way dialog, not silent reception.
⬜
1
Ask Discovery Questions
Ask questions that promote insight and new thinking — not yes/no questions. Create two-way dialog that's constructive for both people.
⬜
2
Build Self-Esteem
Interactions should lift the other person. Create a safe environment where they feel comfortable sharing issues and differences without judgment.
⬜
3
Co-operative Conversation
Feedback flows both directions. Challenge or disagree without making the other person defensive. You can have a different view and still be kind.
⬜
4
Make Skillful Suggestions
Offer ideas that open up alternative paths rather than closing conversation down. Suggestions should expand options, not push an agenda.
📶 6 Levels of Listening — tap to check off
1
Create a safe environment to discuss
The other person needs to feel safe enough to be honest. No interrupting, no eye-rolling, no judgment.
⬜
2
Clear away distractions and make eye contact
Phone down. Body turned toward them. Eyes on the person, not the room.
⬜
3
Understand the substance — ask and confirm
Don't assume you understood. Reflect back what you heard. Ask follow-up questions to deepen understanding.
⬜
4
Observe non-verbal clues — 80% of communication
What does their posture say? Their face? Their pace of speech? The words are only 20% of the message.
⬜
5
Understand emotions and feelings — empathize
Don't just hear the content; feel what they're feeling. Empathy isn't agreeing — it's understanding.
⬜
6
Help the other person see the issue in a different light
The highest level of listening. You help them reframe, see new options, and feel less stuck — without telling them what to do.

🧘 Body Calming Strategies
Tap any card to see full instructions. Your body is trying to help you — these strategies work WITH it. Use one when you feel big feelings building up.
Quick Sensory Resets
🙌
Sit On Your Hands
Tap for details
Sit down and place both hands flat under your thighs. Feel the pressure and warmth. Breathe slowly while you count to 10. The weight and pressure gives your nervous system something to focus on.
🤐
Squeeze Lips & Swallow Words
Tap for details
Press your lips together firmly. Swallow any words that might come out too hot right now. Take a slow breath through your nose. This gives your body a moment before reacting.
✊
Squeeze a Fist or Object
Tap for details
Make a tight fist or squeeze a stress ball / putty. Hold for 5 seconds. Release slowly. Repeat 3-5 times. The physical squeeze gives your body a safe place to put the big feeling.
👁️
Squeeze Your Eyes Shut
Tap for details
Close your eyes tightly. Scrunch up your whole face — nose, cheeks, forehead. Hold for 3 seconds. Release completely. The contrast between tension and release is calming to the nervous system.
💬
Positive Self-Talk
Tap for details
Say quietly (or in your head): "I can do this." "This feeling will pass." "I am safe." "I am in control of my choices." Choose your own phrase and repeat it like a quiet chant. Words change body states.
🖐️
5-Finger Breathing
Tap for details
Hold one hand out flat. Use the other hand's pointer finger to trace: breathe IN as you trace up each finger, breathe OUT as you trace down. One full hand = five slow, complete breaths.
Deeper Strategies
🦅
Hawk Hug
Tap for details
Cross your arms across your chest and hold your shoulders. Breathe in and out slowly. Pretend your arms are wings — flap them gently against your shoulders, then give yourself a final hug to finish. The cross-body motion is deeply regulating.
🔥
Fire Hands
Tap for details
Rub your hands together like you're trying to start a fire. Start slow, speed up, then slow back down as you feel the warmth. Focus on the heat and sensation. As your hands warm up, your breathing naturally slows.
❤️
Hand Over Heart
Tap for details
Place your hand flat over your heart. Try to feel your heartbeat. Take 3 slow, deep breaths and feel your chest rise and fall. Connecting to your heartbeat anchors you in your body and slows your heart rate.
🧍
Shoulder Shrug
Tap for details
Sitting or standing, raise your shoulders slowly toward your ears. Pause at the top. Lower them slowly. Breathe out on the way down. Try raising one shoulder at a time. Repeat 5 times. Releases tension held in the neck and upper back.
💺
Chair Push-Ups
Tap for details
Sit in a chair. Hold the seat firmly under your legs. Push your bottom and legs up off the chair. Hold for 3 seconds. Lower slowly. Repeat 5-8 times. The proprioceptive input (pushing and pressing) is calming for the nervous system.
🤲
Open Shut Them
Tap for details
Clench both fists tightly. Slowly release and stretch fingers wide. Then do the same with your feet (in shoes or out). Then your eyes. Feel each body part tense up and let go. Finish by breathing out everything remaining.
